The Cross-Bronx Expressway was connected to other places within the Bronx by internal and causal relationships, suggesting that the Expressway had a distinct impact on the region as a whole.

One of the main things that the map shows is the connection between the most severe economic areas of impact from the Cross-Bronx Expressway and the areas with the most affected housing stock from the fires of the 1970s. Notably, the areas with the most economic consequences from the Expressway were the areas that later engaged in "planned shrinkage", the purposeful closure of essential municipal infrastructure (such as firehouses) to push people away from an area. This demonstrates that the consequences of the Expressway were direct and necessary predecessors to the conditions that created the arson epidemic in the Bronx.

Another important aspect of the map is the overlap of the polygon that displays fires and high-flight areas, the polygon displaying the worst singular fire event, and the polygon that shows the Foxhurst area: the region that was under the "protection" of the 41st Precinct. The 41st Precinct famously had the attitude of it being them versus the region, and policed in a way that was consistent with that attitude. However, despite their intensity and often extrajudicial actions, the worst of the fires took place within that region, proving the importance of municipal and social infrastructure.

## Data and credits
One of the best sources for this project has been Born in Flames by Bench Ansfield. It is one of the definitive works of scholarship on the Bronx fires, and has been immensely helpful in guiding my research. I have also used The Powerbroker by Robert Caro, a fantastic history of New York infrastructure and biography of Robert Moses in one. Decade of Fire, a documentary on the Bronx fires, was also used. Beyond these three larger sources, I've read many articles from a variety of sources (the best of which will be included in the resources tab), as well as accessing digitized lists and census data to assist with putting numbers to estimated locations and tracking municipal closures. 

## What my map cannot show
Something that the map is incapable of showing is where people went after the fires. Due to the chaos of the time and the fact that many families were displaced multiple times, often in fairly quick succession, the records of where people went and settled permanently are incredibly limited. This makes it very difficult to accurately map the movements of people displaced by the fires.
